Day A · POOL — Technique & Freestyle
Set your own distances · tap any distance to edit · saves automatically
Stroke checklist
Body rotation — rotate hips and torso 30–45° with each pull; swimming flat loses power.
High elbow catch — as the hand extends, bend the elbow so the forearm points down while the elbow stays near the surface. Pull against the water, don't slice through it.
Long reach — reach fully forward on entry and let the hand glide before the catch.
High elbow recovery — lift the elbow and let the fingertips trail close to the surface on the way forward.
Kick from the hip — loose ankles, soft knees, short fast kicks.
Breathing — every 3 strokes, turning with the body roll, one goggle in the water. Exhale fully underwater.
WARM-UP
Kickboard kick
m ×
Technique
Setup — Hold the far end of the board with straight arms, face in the water or chin resting on the surface.

Do it — Kick from the hip with long, loose legs — knees soft, ankles floppy, toes pointed. Short fast kicks, heels just breaking the surface.

Watch for — Bending at the knee (a bicycle kick), big splashy kicks, hips sinking. Press your chest down slightly to lift the hips.

Why — Hips at the surface cut drag; a hip-driven kick is what holds your body position over 600m.
Easy freestyle
m ×
Technique
Do it — Breathe every 3 strokes, alternating sides. Turn your head with your body roll, keeping one goggle in the water. Exhale steadily through nose and mouth while your face is down so you only need to inhale when you turn. Reach long on entry, hand in line with your shoulder.

Watch for — Lifting your head to breathe (your hips drop) and holding your breath underwater.

Why — Easy effort to settle breathing and rhythm before the work.
DRILL BLOCK
Catch-up drill
🤿 Snorkel optional
m ×
Technique
Do it — Keep one arm extended in front until the other hand arrives and touches it, then start that arm's pull. Kick steadily throughout. Breathe to the side of the pulling arm.

Watch for — Dropping the lead hand early, or crossing the centre line on entry.

Why — Trains a long stroke and patience at the front — more distance per stroke. A snorkel lets you focus only on the timing.
LEG STRENGTH DRILL
Vertical kick
sec ×
Technique
Setup — Deep water, body upright, arms crossed on your chest. Harder: hands out of the water.

Do it — Flutter kick from the hips — small and fast — to keep your chin above the water for the set time.

Watch for — Cycling from the knees. If you're sinking, scull with your hands rather than stopping.

Why — Pure leg power, and the same skill as treading water in the PCA test.
Kickboard sprint
m ×
Technique
Setup — Same position as kickboard kick.

Do it — Maximum effort kick for the distance, then full recovery (30–60 sec) before the next rep.

Watch for — Technique falling apart into knee kicks as you tire — keep it from the hip.

Why — Builds kick speed and power.
FREESTYLE SET
Freestyle intervals
m ×
Technique
Do it — Moderate effort, bilateral breathing, 20–30 sec rest between reps. Aim for the same time on every rep.

Watch for — The stroke checklist at the top of Day A — rotation, high elbow catch, long reach. If the last rep is much slower than the first, you started too fast.

Why — Builds aerobic fitness while holding good technique.
Continuous freestyle
m ×
Technique
Do it — One continuous swim at a comfortable pace you can hold. Same stroke rhythm as the intervals.

Watch for — Speeding up in the first lengths. If you need a breather, breathe every 2 strokes for a length rather than stopping.

Why — This is the PCA test skill — steady pace without stopping.

Snorkel freestyle
🤿 Snorkel
m ×
Technique
Setup — Centre-mount snorkel, strap snug.

Do it — Head still, eyes on the bottom. Focus on rotating from the hips and a high elbow catch. Exhale steadily through your mouth into the snorkel; clear it with one sharp blow.

Watch for — Lifting your head or rocking side to side.

Why — Takes breathing out of the stroke so you can work purely on rotation and arm mechanics.
Hand paddles
🏓 Paddles🔴 + Pull Buoy
m ×
Technique
Setup — Start with small paddles and the pull buoy.

Do it — Slow deliberate strokes. Enter fingertips first — no slapping. Catch with a high elbow and push the water back, not down, through to your hip.

Watch for — Any shoulder pain or pinching — stop. Paddles add load to the shoulder, so with your scapula and rotator cuff work keep these sets short.

Why — You feel exactly where your pull is (and isn't) holding water.
Pull buoy
🔴 Pull Buoy
m ×
Technique
Setup — Buoy between your upper thighs, legs together, no kick. Cross your ankles if they drift apart.

Do it — Arms only. Let your body roll drive the stroke and focus on catch, pull and push.

Watch for — Hips swaying side to side — keep your core tight.

Why — Isolates the arms and builds upper-body swimming strength.
FINS BLOCK
Continuous freestyle with fins
🦈 Fins
m ×
Technique
Setup — Short-blade fins.

Do it — Continuous swim, still kicking from the hip. Let the fins lift your hips and keep the stroke long.

Watch for — Your stroke shortening because the fins make you fast, or technique dropping in the second half.

Why — More distance at good technique, and endurance at swim pace.

TREADING WATER
longest hold
Technique

BIKE RIDE
3–5 min
Technique
Setup — Seat at hip height so there's a slight bend in the knee at the bottom.

Do it — 3–5 min easy to moderate, around 80–90 rpm.

Why — Raises heart rate and warms the joints before loading.
THORACIC ROTATIONS
2×10 each
Technique
Setup — Kneel and sit back on your heels (locks the lower back), hands behind your head.

Do it — Rotate your upper back to one side, exhale as you turn, return and switch. 2×10 each side.

Watch for — Hips or lower back twisting — the movement should come from between the shoulder blades.

Why — Frees the upper back stiffness that shift work and driving build up.
BODYWEIGHT SQUATS
2×10
Technique
Setup — Feet shoulder-width, toes slightly out.

Do it — Sit hips back and down, chest up, heels flat. Pause 1 sec at the bottom, then stand.

Watch for — Knees caving in or heels lifting.

Why — Primes quads, glutes and hips before loading.
LEGS · ~15 min
WEIGHTED STEP-UPS
25 reps × 2
Technique
Setup — 50 cm box, dumbbells at your sides or a weighted vest.

Do it — Whole foot on the box. Drive through the heel of the top leg to stand fully tall, then lower slowly (2–3 sec). Alternate legs.

Watch for — Pushing off the bottom foot, or the knee caving inward.

Why — Single-leg strength for the kick and for lifting and carrying at work.

STANDING CALF RAISES
3×15
Technique
Setup — Balls of your feet on the edge of a step, hand on a wall for balance.

Do it — Lower your heels below the step, rise as high as you can, pause 1 sec, lower over 2 sec. Knees straight.

Watch for — Bouncing at the bottom or cutting the range short.

Why — Ankle strength and range — important for a strong, pointed-toe kick.

ROMANIAN DEADLIFT
3×10
Technique
Setup — Barbell or dumbbells, feet hip-width, soft knees.

Do it — Send your hips back and lower the weight down the front of your legs, back flat, until you feel a strong hamstring stretch. Drive the hips forward to stand. 3 sec down, 60 sec rest.

Watch for — Rounding the back, bending the knees into a squat, or the weight drifting away from your legs.

Why — Hamstrings and the hip hinge behind kick recovery and posterior chain strength.
No previous entry
BULGARIAN SPLIT SQUAT
3×6 each
Technique
Setup — Stand about two foot-lengths in front of a bench, laces of the back foot on it.

Do it — Lower the back knee toward the floor with the front heel down, then drive through the front heel to stand. 60 sec rest.

Watch for — Front knee caving in, or a stride so short the front heel lifts.

Why — Unilateral strength without the other leg compensating, plus a hip flexor stretch under load that helps kick range.

SHOULDER REHABILITATION · ~15 min
CABLE EXT. ROTATION
2×12 each
Technique
Setup — Cable at waist height, stand side-on. Rolled towel between your elbow and ribs, elbow bent 90°.

Do it — Rotate the forearm out away from your body, pause, return over 3 sec. Low or no weight.

Watch for — The towel dropping or your trunk turning — the forearm should be the only thing moving.

Why — Strengthens infraspinatus and teres minor to stabilise the shoulder.

CABLE EXT. ROT. 90°
2×12 each
Technique
Setup — Cable at shoulder height. Upper arm out to the side at 90°, elbow bent 90°, forearm pointing forward.

Do it — Rotate the forearm up toward the ceiling, pause, return over 3 sec. Low weight.

Watch for — The elbow dropping below shoulder height, or any pinching at the front of the shoulder.

Why — Trains the rotator cuff in the overhead position of the swimming catch.

YTW RAISES
2×8 each
Technique
Setup — Face-down on an incline bench, light plates or no weight, neck neutral looking at the floor.

Do it — Y — arms overhead in a Y, thumbs up, 2 sec hold. T — arms straight out to the sides, 2 sec hold. W — elbows bent 90°, pull back and squeeze, 2 sec hold. All three shapes, then rest 45 sec.

Watch for — Shrugging toward your ears — draw the shoulder blades down and back first.

Why — Y works the lower trapezius, T and W the middle trapezius and rhomboids — the muscles that hold the shoulder blade flat.

Do it — Keep the elbow straight and push the dumbbell toward the ceiling so the shoulder blade lifts off the floor (only a few cm), pause, lower.

Watch for — Bending the elbow — the shoulder blade is the only thing that moves.

Why — Serratus anterior holds the shoulder blade against the ribs; weakness here is what lets it wing.

CORE · ~10 min
PALLOF PRESS
2×10 each
Technique
Cable or band at chest height. Stand sideways to the stack. Hold handle at chest, brace core hard, feet shoulder width. Press handle straight out — hold 2 sec — return to chest. The cable tries to pull you into rotation; your core resists it. This is anti-rotation — the most swimming-specific core pattern. Complete all reps one side then turn and repeat. 45 sec rest between sides.
No previous entry
DEAD BUG
2×10 each
Technique
Setup — On your back, arms straight up, hips and knees at 90°.

Do it — Exhale and slowly lower the opposite arm and leg toward the floor, keeping your lower back pressed down. Return and switch. 2×10 each.

Watch for — Your lower back arching off the floor — shorten the range until it stays down.

Why — Core control while the limbs move, as in swimming.
SIDE PLANK
2×30 sec each
Technique
Setup — On your side, elbow directly under your shoulder, feet stacked.

Do it — Lift your hips so your body is a straight line from head to feet. Hold, breathing normally. Log your hold time. Easier: knees bent.

Watch for — Hips sagging or rolling forwards.

Why — Lateral core and hip strength for stable body rotation.

STRETCHING · ~5 min
ESSENTIAL 3
Technique
Hip flexor lunge — 45s each side. Non-negotiable: tight hip flexors directly cap your kick range of motion.
Pec doorway stretch — 45s. Swimmers are chronically internally rotated — this counteracts it.
Child's pose — 45s. Thoracic extension and lat release. Counteracts shift work rounding.

Everything else — hamstring, glute, lat, neck — do at home or poolside on swim days.

Day C · OCEAN SWIM
Open water session · check conditions first

⚠️ SAFETY FIRST — CHECK BEFORE EVERY OCEAN SWIM

  • Check surf report — avoid conditions above your current ability
  • Always swim with a buddy or tell someone your plan and return time
  • Wear a brightly coloured swim cap — visibility matters
  • Attach an inflatable tow float for open water
  • Know the rip current escape — swim parallel to shore, not against it
  • Check for marine stingers in your area before entering
Open water skills
Sighting — "crocodile eyes" every 6–10 strokes, two strokes between sights; never lift the whole head.
Swimming straight — aim slightly up-current of your target; bilateral breathing prevents one-sided drift.
Chop and swell — shorter, quicker stroke; breathe away from the swell; stay calm if you swallow water.
Currents — watch the water for 2 minutes before you go in. Swim across a rip, never against it.
Pacing — start slower than you think; same effort in the second half as the first.
Disorientation without lane lines is normal and fades with exposure — regular ocean sessions build the confidence for the PCA test.

Why — Gets you comfortable in the water before the work.

LANDMARK SIGHT
Technique

Watch for — Lifting your whole head (your hips sink). Without lane lines a 20–30° drift is normal — bilateral breathing and regular sighting keep you straight.

Why — Open water navigation.

Salt vs Sodium on labels: These are not the same number. Salt is sodium chloride — it is 40% sodium by weight. So 1g of salt = 400mg sodium. If a label shows "sodium 600mg" that equals about 1.5g of salt. Most NZ and Australian labels list sodium — multiply by 2.5 to convert to salt equivalent. Under 600mg sodium (1.5g salt) per serving is the guide.
Hidden sources: Processed foods are the main culprit — bread, sauces, soups, deli meats. Cook from scratch where you can and you automatically reduce sodium significantly.

ICE BATH
Once per week · After pool sessions
HOW TO USE IT
Temperature10–12°C
Duration3–5 minutes per session
Frequency11 min combined total per week — 2 × 5–6 min sessions
BENEFITS ▼
Noradrenaline surge (up to 300%) — the primary driver of improved mood, focus and stress resilience. Comparable in magnitude to some antidepressant medications in its neurochemical effect.

Vagus nerve activation — shifts the nervous system toward parasympathetic (recovery) state. Accelerates recovery from the cortisol load of night shifts.

Inflammation and DOMS reduction — reduces systemic inflammatory markers. Useful for recovery between training sessions.

Psychological hardening — repeated voluntary exposure to controlled discomfort retrains the stress response threshold over time.
STRESS MANAGEMENT ▼
The ice bath is a controlled, predictable stressor. Learning to breathe steadily and remain calm while your body demands you get out is identical to the cognitive skill required in high-pressure situations — a trauma call, the Heli PCA test under fatigue. Every session where you stay in and regulate your breathing is a direct training rep for stress management. The confidence this builds transfers.

COLD SHOWERS
Daily · Low barrier, sustainable, high return
HOW TO USE IT
TemperatureAs cold as your shower goes
Duration3 minutes of cold at the end of your normal shower
BENEFITS ▼
Sustained dopamine elevation (up to 250%) — unlike caffeine, cold-induced dopamine is a prolonged stable rise, not a spike and crash. Powerful for motivation and mood on the back end of night shifts.

Sympathetic then parasympathetic rebound — the calm, alert state that follows cold is why a cold shower before an early shift outperforms coffee alone.

Habituates the stress response — daily practice gradually raises your threshold for anxiety reactivity. Things that triggered stress increasingly do not.
STRESS MANAGEMENT ▼
Every time cold hits and you override the urge to gasp with slow breathing, you are practising the identical cognitive skill required to stay composed under pressure. Done daily, the gap between stimulus and response grows wider. Use before every shift and before every training session.

SAUNA
Once per week · Powerful recovery and cardiovascular tool
HOW TO USE IT
Duration15–20 min per session
FrequencyTwice per week
Hydration500ml water before, 500ml after — non-negotiable
Best protocolSauna 15–20 min → cold shower 1–2 min → rest 10 min → repeat 2–3 cycles
BENEFITS ▼
Cardiovascular conditioning — heart rate during sauna sits at 120–150 bpm, equivalent to moderate aerobic exercise. Directly supports swimming endurance with no physical loading cost.

Plasma volume expansion — increases blood plasma volume, improving oxygen delivery to working muscles and swimming performance.

Growth hormone release — increases up to 16× with repeated sauna/cool cycles. Supports muscle growth relevant to your strength goals.

Resting cortisol reduction — regular sauna measurably lowers baseline cortisol. For a shift worker carrying chronic stress load this compounds significantly over weeks.
STRESS MANAGEMENT ▼
Sauna after a stressful shift accelerates recovery in a way passive rest does not. The post-sauna parasympathetic rebound resets the nervous system. The hot/cold contrast protocol trains rapid switching between activation and recovery states — which over weeks builds a nervous system that is more responsive when needed and faster to return to baseline when the threat has passed.

BOX BREATHING
Daily · Immediate acute stress control · No equipment
HOW TO USE IT
PatternInhale 4 sec → hold 4 sec → exhale 4 sec → hold 4 sec. Repeat 4–6 cycles
Duration2–3 minutes is enough for measurable effect
BENEFITS ▼
Immediate parasympathetic activation — the extended exhale and breath hold lower heart rate and cortisol within 2 minutes. This is a direct physiological override of the stress response via the vagus nerve.

Used by military and emergency services — tactical breathing is standard training for Navy SEALs, paramedics and firefighters because it works under extreme acute stress without equipment or preparation.

Improves CO2 tolerance — extended holds raise your threshold for the discomfort of breath-holding, which directly transfers to swimming and reduces the urge to breathe prematurely.
STRESS MANAGEMENT ▼
Box breathing is the most immediately applicable tool in this section. Use it in the car before a difficult shift, in the water before the start of the Heli PCA swim, or in bed after a traumatic job. The ability to deliberately slow your physiology under acute stress is a learnable skill — this is the most direct way to practise it.
