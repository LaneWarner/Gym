⚠️ SAFETY FIRST — CHECK BEFORE EVERY OCEAN SWIM

  • Check surf report — avoid conditions above your current ability
  • Always swim with a buddy or tell someone your plan and return time
  • Wear a brightly coloured swim cap — visibility matters
  • Attach an inflatable tow float for open water

  • Check for marine stingers in your area before entering

FREESTYLE SWIMMING TECHNIQUE▼
Bilateral breathing▼
Every 3 strokes, alternate sides. Rotate your head only — not your body. Exhale fully through nose and mouth underwater between breaths.
Body rotation▼
Rotate hips and torso as each arm pulls — this is where power comes from. Aim for 30–45° rotation each stroke. Swimming flat loses significant propulsive force.
High elbow catch (EVF)▼
As lead hand extends forward, bend the elbow so the forearm points down while elbow stays near the surface. Creates a vertical forearm to pull against — not slice through. Fingertip drag drill builds this.
Kick from the hip▼
Drive from the hip — not the knee. Ankles loose, knees soft. Short, fast kicks. Wide bicycle kicks waste energy. Vertical kick drill builds this directly.
Long stroke and glide▼
Reach fully forward on entry. Let hand glide before catching. Catch-up drill trains this — pause the trailing hand until the lead hand has fully extended.
Fingertip drag recovery▼
During arm recovery, drag fingertips along the surface to keep the elbow high. High elbow recovery sets up the correct entry angle for the EVF catch.
Snorkel training▼
Removes breathing so you focus entirely on rotation, head position and arm mechanics. Drill blocks only — main sets need breathing practice.
OPEN WATER SWIMMING TECHNIQUE▼
Sighting▼
Every 6–10 strokes, lift eyes just above waterline — spot landmark — face back down immediately. Never lift the full head — hips sink. Two strokes between sights.
Swimming straight▼
Without lane lines, drift of 20–30° is common. Sight a fixed landmark every 6 strokes. Aim slightly upshore of your target to compensate for any current. Bilateral breathing prevents one-sided drift.
Managing chop and swell▼
Shorten stroke, increase rate. Breathe away from the swell direction. Sight every 4 strokes in heavy chop. Stay calm if you swallow water — it passes.
Current awareness▼
Observe the water for 2 minutes before entering. Swim across a rip — never against it. In a current, aim upshore so it carries you to your target.
Beach entry and exit▼
Time your entry between sets. Duck-dive through wave bases — not over them. To exit, bodysurf the last wave in then stand as soon as it's shallow. Don't swim through shallow breaking surf.
Pacing▼
Start slower than you think. Second half should match first half effort. If you are working harder to maintain pace, you started too fast.
Key differences from pool▼
Same stroke mechanics, no reference points. Disorientation is normal — it reduces with exposure. Regular ocean sessions build the confidence needed for the Heli PCA test.
GYM EXERCISE GUIDE — DAYS B & D▼
LEGS
Weighted Step-Ups▼
Full foot on box. Drive through the heel — not the toe. Stand tall at the top. Controlled descent. Don't push off the trailing foot.
Romanian Deadlift▼
Soft knee, back flat. Hinge from the hip — send hips back as weight lowers down the front of your legs. Stop at strong hamstring stretch. Drive hips forward to stand. 3-sec descent.
Bulgarian Split Squat▼

SHOULDER REHABILITATION

One arm vertical, light DB. Press past vertical — scapula should lift off the floor at the top. Slow. This is the primary serratus anterior exercise.
Cable Ext. Rotation (arm at side)▼
Upper arm pinned to torso, elbow 90°. Only the forearm rotates outward. Low or no weight. If the upper arm lifts away, you're compensating — reduce weight.
Cable Ext. Rotation at 90°▼
Upper arm parallel to floor, elbow 90°. Rotate forearm upward toward the ceiling — only the forearm moves. Low weight, precision only.
DB Ext. Rotation — side-lying (Day D)▼
Towel between elbow and ribs. Only the forearm rotates up. If the elbow lifts off the towel or you roll backwards, the weight is too heavy.
DB Ext. Rotation at 90° (Day D)▼
Upper arm supported at shoulder height. Forearm starts pointing forward and rotates up to vertical. Slow down, slow up. Very light.
YTW Raises▼
Face-down on incline bench. Y — arms overhead, thumbs up, 2-sec hold. T — arms wide, 2-sec hold. W — elbows 90° pulled back, 2-sec hold. All three shapes before resting.
UPPER BODY
Chest Press▼
Elbows max 45° from torso. Pause at chest. Press explosively. Core braced — don't arch off the bench. Move straight to rows in the superset.
Bent-Over Rows▼
Back flat, hinge at hips. Pull DBs to lower ribs — elbows back, not out. Squeeze shoulder blades together at top. Lower slowly.
Lat Pulldown▼
Wide grip. Pull to upper chest, elbows drive down and back. Don't let shoulders shrug on the return — lower with full control.
Shoulder Press▼
Forearms directly vertical and in line with the trunk throughout — not angled forward or back. Press to full lockout overhead.
Push-Ups▼
Chest within 2cm of floor. Body straight throughout — no sagging or piking. Go to failure on the last set.
CORE
Pallof Press▼
Stand sideways to cable. Hold handle at chest, brace hard. Press straight out — hold 2 sec — return. The core resists rotation. Bracing is the exercise, not the pressing.
Dead Bug▼
Lower back flat to the floor throughout. Opposite arm and leg lower simultaneously. If the back lifts, you've gone too far — reduce range.
Side Plank▼
Hips don't sag. Body straight from head to feet. Squeeze glutes. Hold for time and log it each session.
STRETCHING — After every gym session▼
Hip flexor lunge · 45s each▼
Kneel, other foot forward. Shift weight forward until hip stretch felt. Trunk upright. Squeeze rear glute to deepen. Switch sides.
Pec doorway stretch · 45s▼
Forearms on door frame at shoulder height, elbows 90°. Step forward until chest stretch felt. Chin tucked — don't let head push forward.
Child's pose · 45s▼
Sit back toward heels, reach arms forward along the floor. Forehead down. Feel the stretch through lats, thoracic spine and shoulders.
Hamstring stretch · 45s each▼
Seated, one leg straight. Hinge forward from the hip — not the lower back — until hamstring stretch felt. Reach toward foot. Switch sides.
Pigeon pose / glute · 60s each▼
Bring knee forward toward wrist, shin angled across body. Lower hips. Rear leg straight behind. Hold and breathe. Switch sides.
Lat side-bend · 30s each▼
One arm overhead, lean to the opposite side. Feet planted. Feel stretch along the side of the torso into the armpit. Switch sides.
Shoulder cross-body · 30s each▼
Arm straight across chest, other hand pulls it in. Feel stretch in the back of the shoulder. Keep shoulder down — don't shrug. Switch sides.
Neck side tilt · 30s each▼
Ear toward shoulder. Face stays forward — don't rotate. Light hand pressure on head to deepen. Switch sides.
Thoracic extension on roller · 45s▼
Roller at mid-back. Lower upper back over it. Move up and down to find tight spots. Hold on tender areas 5–10 sec each.

ICE BATH
Once per week · After pool sessions
HOW TO USE IT
Temperature10–12°C
Duration3–5 minutes per session
Frequency11 min combined total per week — 2 × 5–6 min sessions
BENEFITS ▼
Noradrenaline surge (up to 300%) — the primary driver of improved mood, focus and stress resilience. Comparable in magnitude to some antidepressant medications in its neurochemical effect.

Vagus nerve activation — shifts the nervous system toward parasympathetic (recovery) state. Accelerates recovery from the cortisol load of night shifts.

Inflammation and DOMS reduction — reduces systemic inflammatory markers. Useful for recovery between training sessions.

Psychological hardening — repeated voluntary exposure to controlled discomfort retrains the stress response threshold over time.
STRESS MANAGEMENT ▼
The ice bath is a controlled, predictable stressor. Learning to breathe steadily and remain calm while your body demands you get out is identical to the cognitive skill required in high-pressure situations — a trauma call, the Heli PCA test under fatigue. Every session where you stay in and regulate your breathing is a direct training rep for stress management. The confidence this builds transfers.
🚿
COLD SHOWERS
Daily · Low barrier, sustainable, high return
HOW TO USE IT
TemperatureAs cold as your shower goes
Duration3 minutes of cold at the end of your normal shower
BENEFITS ▼
Sustained dopamine elevation (up to 250%) — unlike caffeine, cold-induced dopamine is a prolonged stable rise, not a spike and crash. Powerful for motivation and mood on the back end of night shifts.

Sympathetic then parasympathetic rebound — the calm, alert state that follows cold is why a cold shower before an early shift outperforms coffee alone.

Habituates the stress response — daily practice gradually raises your threshold for anxiety reactivity. Things that triggered stress increasingly do not.
STRESS MANAGEMENT ▼
Every time cold hits and you override the urge to gasp with slow breathing, you are practising the identical cognitive skill required to stay composed under pressure. Done daily, the gap between stimulus and response grows wider. Use before every shift and before every training session.

Cardiovascular conditioning — heart rate during sauna sits at 120–150 bpm, equivalent to moderate aerobic exercise. Directly supports swimming endurance with no physical loading cost.

Plasma volume expansion — increases blood plasma volume, improving oxygen delivery to working muscles and swimming performance.

Growth hormone release — increases up to 16× with repeated sauna/cool cycles. Supports muscle growth relevant to your strength goals.

Resting cortisol reduction — regular sauna measurably lowers baseline cortisol. For a shift worker carrying chronic stress load this compounds significantly over weeks.
STRESS MANAGEMENT ▼
Sauna after a stressful shift accelerates recovery in a way passive rest does not. The post-sauna parasympathetic rebound resets the nervous system. The hot/cold contrast protocol trains rapid switching between activation and recovery states — which over weeks builds a nervous system that is more responsive when needed and faster to return to baseline when the threat has passed.

BOX BREATHING
Daily · Immediate acute stress control · No equipment
HOW TO USE IT
PatternInhale 4 sec → hold 4 sec → exhale 4 sec → hold 4 sec. Repeat 4–6 cycles
Duration2–3 minutes is enough for measurable effect
BENEFITS ▼
Immediate parasympathetic activation — the extended exhale and breath hold lower heart rate and cortisol within 2 minutes. This is a direct physiological override of the stress response via the vagus nerve.

Used by military and emergency services — tactical breathing is standard training for Navy SEALs, paramedics and firefighters because it works under extreme acute stress without equipment or preparation.

Improves CO2 tolerance — extended holds raise your threshold for the discomfort of breath-holding, which directly transfers to swimming and reduces the urge to breathe prematurely.
STRESS MANAGEMENT ▼
Box breathing is the most immediately applicable tool in this section. Use it in the car before a difficult shift, in the water before the start of the Heli PCA swim, or in bed after a traumatic job. The ability to deliberately slow your physiology under acute stress is a learnable skill — this is the most direct way to practise it.
